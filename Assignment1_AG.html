The signs reverse because AFT and PH models parameterize effects differently: AFT (Accelerated Failure Time):

Models the effect on survival time Positive coefficient → longer survival time (slower failure) Example: clinic coefficient = +0.698 means Clinic 2 patients survive longer

PH (Proportional Hazards):

Models the effect on hazard rate (risk of failure) Positive coefficient → higher hazard (faster failure) Example: clinic coefficient = -0.961 means Clinic 2 patients have lower hazard

The relationship: Longer survival time ↔︎ Lower hazard rate So a positive AFT coefficient (longer survival) corresponds to a negative PH coefficient (lower hazard), and vice versa. They’re expressing the same relationship from opposite perspectives.

Interpretation Question 4: Why are these survival curves marginal?

The survival curves are marginal because of Step 5 (averaging cumulative survival probabilities across all individuals). This step averages the predicted survival probabilities over the entire study population for each time point and education level. By doing this, we are standardizing (or weighting) the estimates according to the observed distribution of all other covariates (heblack, mixed) in the actual study population. In other words, the marginal survival curves represent what would happen if we could set everyone’s education to a specific level while maintaining their actual values for race and mixed-race status. This creates population-averaged estimates that account for the covariate distribution, rather than conditional estimates for specific covariate combinations.
